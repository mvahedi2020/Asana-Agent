# GTM Strategy

This is a portfolio sample, not a launched product. Its hypothetical **user** is a product, delivery, or program lead who needs to inspect work and prepare a routine update without losing the record. Its hypothetical **buyer** could be an operations or product leader accountable for reliable coordination across a team. The user needs a clear task and safe recovery; the buyer would need governance, access, auditability, and confidence that the layer improves a real task system. No one has been interviewed for either role.

Two paths could be tested after discovery. An internal design-partner pilot could position the workflow as “inspectable, reversible updates” beside an existing task system. A different path could be a standalone learning sandbox for teams introducing conversational assistance. In either case, guided opt-in demos are more appropriate than a broad launch. A hypothetical activation event is completing the Northstar NTH-104 status update while correctly explaining its preview; it is a learning signal, not a conversion metric.

An illustrative adoption threshold is four of five consented target users completing the proposed safety tasks without a critical action-boundary error. Retention would require repeated, voluntary use of briefing and update flows, but no retention study is planned or reported here. No waitlist, outreach, partner, marketplace approval, price, conversion, adoption, or revenue result exists.

Before any production investment, permissions, tenant isolation, audit history, integration reliability, privacy, support, and model evaluation need separate evidence-backed decisions. The prototype does not establish that a conversational interface is preferable to direct task controls.
